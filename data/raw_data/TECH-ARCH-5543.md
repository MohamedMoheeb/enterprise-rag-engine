## 1. Document Scope and Executive Summary

This document outlines the key policies, standard operating procedures (SOPs), and guidelines for the Architecture department. The scope of this document includes all aspects of architecture development, maintenance, and governance within the organization.

The primary objectives of this document are:

* To establish a framework for architecture development that aligns with organizational goals
* To define the roles and responsibilities of architecture team members
* To provide guidance on best practices and standards for architecture development
* To outline the procedures for compliance monitoring, penalties, and operational governance

This document applies to all employees involved in architecture development, including architects, designers, engineers, and project managers.


## 2. Core Policies and Standard Operating Procedures

### 2.1 Architecture Development Life Cycle

The architecture development life cycle consists of the following stages:

* Conceptualization: Identify business requirements and define the scope of the architecture
* Design: Develop a high-level design for the architecture
* Implementation: Implement the architecture based on the approved design
* Testing: Test the implemented architecture to ensure it meets the required standards
* Deployment: Deploy the tested architecture into production
* Maintenance: Maintain the deployed architecture through regular updates and enhancements

### 2.2 Architecture Review Board (ARB)

The ARB is responsible for reviewing and approving all architecture development projects within the organization. The ARB will review the following:

* Architecture design documents
* High-level designs
* Implementation plans
* Test plans
* Deployment plans

The ARB will ensure that all architecture development projects align with organizational goals, are well-defined, and meet the required standards.

### 2.3 Change Management Process

The change management process is used to manage changes to existing architectures within the organization. The process consists of the following stages:

* Request: Submit a request for change
* Assessment: Assess the impact of the proposed change on existing architectures
* Approval: Obtain approval from the ARB or other authorized personnel
* Implementation: Implement the approved change

### 2.4 Architecture Governance Framework

The architecture governance framework outlines the roles and responsibilities of architecture team members, as well as the procedures for compliance monitoring, penalties, and operational governance.

#### Roles and Responsibilities

* Architecture Team Lead: Oversees all architecture development projects within the organization
* Architects: Develop and maintain architectures within the organization
* Designers: Assist architects in developing and maintaining architectures
* Engineers: Implement architectures based on approved designs
* Project Managers: Manage architecture development projects from start to finish

#### Compliance Monitoring, Penalties, and Operational Governance

The organization will conduct regular compliance monitoring to ensure that all architecture development projects align with organizational goals and meet the required standards. Non-compliance may result in penalties, including but not limited to:

* Delayed project timelines
* Rejection of project submissions
* Suspension or termination of project managers or team members


## 3. Compliance Monitoring, Penalties, and Operational Governance

The organization will conduct regular compliance monitoring to ensure that all architecture development projects align with organizational goals and meet the required standards.

### 3.1 Compliance Monitoring Process

The compliance monitoring process consists of the following stages:

* Review: Conduct a review of architecture development projects to ensure they align with organizational goals and meet the required standards
* Assessment: Assess the results of the review to determine if any non-compliance issues have been identified
* Investigation: Investigate any identified non-compliance issues to determine root cause and corrective actions

### 3.2 Penalties for Non-Compliance

Non-compliance with organizational policies, procedures, or standards may result in penalties, including but not limited to:

* Delayed project timelines
* Rejection of project submissions
* Suspension or termination of project managers or team members
