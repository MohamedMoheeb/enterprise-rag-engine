## 2. Core Policies and Standard Operating Procedures

### 2.1 Architecture Decision-Making Process

The architecture decision-making process is a formalized approach to making informed decisions about the technical direction of the company. This process involves:

1. Problem identification: Clearly define the problem or opportunity that requires an architectural solution.
2. Options evaluation: Evaluate potential solutions and their implications on the business, technology, and stakeholders.
3. Recommendation: Present a recommended solution based on the analysis and evaluation.
4. Approval: Obtain approval from relevant stakeholders and management.

### 2.2 Architectural Design Standards

The Architecture department will adhere to the following design standards:

* Use of standard architectural frameworks (e.g., TOGAF, Zachman)
* Adherence to industry-standard naming conventions
* Consistent use of architecture description languages (ADLs) for documentation and communication
* Compliance with security and privacy regulations (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA)

### 2.3 Design Review Process

The design review process is a critical step in ensuring that architectural designs meet the company's technical and business requirements. This process involves:

1. Design submission: Architects submit their designs for review.
2. Peer review: Peers review designs and provide feedback on architecture, scalability, security, and maintainability.
3. Managerial review: Managers review designs and provide final approval or feedback.

## 3. Compliance Monitoring, Penalties, and Operational Governance

### 3.1 Compliance Monitoring

The Architecture department will monitor compliance with industry standards, regulations, and internal policies through:

* Regular audits and assessments
* Quarterly reviews of architectural designs and documentation
* Annual reports on compliance status to management and the board of directors

### 3.2 Penalties for Non-Compliance

 Failure to comply with architecture standards, regulations, or internal policies may result in:

* Reprimand or warning
* Mandatory training or education
* Suspension or termination of architectural privileges

### 3.3 Operational Governance

The Architecture department will adhere to the following operational governance principles:

* Transparency: Clearly document and communicate design decisions and reasoning.
* Accountability: Hold architects accountable for their designs and decisions.
* Consistency: Ensure consistency in design approaches, naming conventions, and documentation.
