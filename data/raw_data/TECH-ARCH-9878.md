## Department: Architecture
## Author: Suzanne Diaz
## Effective Date: 2026-01-11

# 1. Document Scope and Executive Summary

This document outlines the reference framework for the Architecture department, providing a comprehensive overview of our policies, procedures, and guidelines for designing, developing, and maintaining enterprise architecture.

The purpose of this document is to establish a unified approach to architecture development, ensuring consistency and alignment across all business units and departments. This document serves as a single source of truth for architecture-related matters, providing clarity and direction to stakeholders, architects, and developers.

# 2. Core Policies and Standard Operating Procedures

### 2.1 Design Principles

* Architecture designs must adhere to the following principles:
	+ Scalability: Designs should be scalable to accommodate growth and changing business needs.
	+ Flexibility: Designs should allow for flexibility in terms of technology stack, infrastructure, and deployment models.
	+ Security: Designs should prioritize security, ensuring confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data.

### 2.2 Architecture Development Process

* The architecture development process consists of the following stages:
	1. Requirements Gathering: Gather requirements from stakeholders and business owners.
	2. Design: Develop a comprehensive architecture design based on gathered requirements.
	3. Review and Approval: Review and approve the design with relevant stakeholders and architects.
	4. Implementation: Implement the approved design, ensuring compliance with established standards and guidelines.

### 2.3 Change Management

* The Architecture department will maintain a change management process for all architecture-related changes, including:
	+ Request for Change (RFC): Submit a formal RFC to propose changes to existing architectures.
	+ Review and Approval: Review and approve proposed changes based on their impact, feasibility, and alignment with business objectives.

# 3. Compliance Monitoring, Penalties, and Operational Governance

### 3.1 Compliance Monitoring

* The Architecture department will monitor compliance with established architecture standards and guidelines, ensuring:
	+ Adherence to design principles and best practices.
	+ Alignment with enterprise-wide strategies and initiatives.
	+ Identification of non-compliant architectures and implementation of corrective actions.

### 3.2 Penalties for Non-Compliance

* In the event of non-compliance, the Architecture department will impose penalties as follows:
	+ Minor infractions: Verbal warnings or minor documentation corrections.
	+ Moderate infractions: Written warnings or temporary suspension of architecture development activities.
	+ Severe infractions: Permanent suspension of architecture development activities or termination of employment.

### 3.3 Operational Governance

* The Architecture department will maintain operational governance through:
	+ Quarterly review and assessment of architecture-related projects and initiatives.
	+ Bi-annual reporting to senior management on compliance, progress, and challenges.
	+ Continuous improvement and refinement of architecture standards and guidelines.
